How much higher will the sea be?

NOAA's 2022 technical report projects relative sea level rise for U.S. coastlines under five scenarios, named for roughly how much global sea level rises by 2100. Local rise differs from the global average because land is also moving: some coasts are sinking, others are rising.

Every number below is a change in centimetres since 2005. Each scenario has a median projection plus a low and high bound of the likely range.

Why does my coast differ from the global number?

Relative sea level combines the ocean rising with the land moving up or down (vertical land motion), plus regional ocean and gravity effects. The Gulf Coast and Mid-Atlantic tend to be higher than the global average; parts of the Pacific Northwest and Alaska can be lower because the land is rising.
